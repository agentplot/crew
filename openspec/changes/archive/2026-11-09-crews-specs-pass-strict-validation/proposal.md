# crew's specs pass strict validation

## Why

`openspec validate --specs --strict` fails 23 of crew's 27 specs. Each fails on one warning alone, which strict counts as a failure: a requirement whose text, above its first scenario, is past the 500 characters openspec allows. 140 requirements are past it, 120,000 characters between them. The verify report of `a-drive-set-loses-no-drive-and-no-record` (archived 2026-11-09) named one of them, drive-setup's "A set is one entry naming what it changed": that change grew it from 326 characters to 613. Nothing in crew's gate runs openspec, so a change whose archive grows a requirement past the limit lands green.

## What Changes

- Every requirement past the limit, in all 23 specs, is removed and added again as requirements that together say the same. Each part keeps the original's sentences and is within the limit. Each holds its SHALL or MUST and the original's scenarios that prove it, every scenario carried word for word, once. No behaviour changes.
- A new end-to-end test, `tests/t-specs.sh`, runs `openspec validate --specs --strict` on crew's checkout. It first proves, on scratch specs of its own, that openspec refuses a requirement of 501 characters and takes one of 500. Its moon task is tagged `check-full`, so sluice runs it on every merge whose change touches `openspec/specs/` (`tests.5`, `tests.10`).
- `openspec/config.yaml`'s spec rules and CLAUDE.md's *Specs* say a requirement's text is at most 500 characters, and that `t-specs` holds every spec to it.
- A requirement that quotes a split one by name quotes the part that holds what it quotes. Every citation of a split requirement's name in `plugin/` and `tests/` names the part that holds what it cites.
